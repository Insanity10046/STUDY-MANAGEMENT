Principle utilised:
1. **First Principle** - In learning, it means understanding the **deep underlying structure** of a subject rather than memorizing surface facts.
2. **Pareto Principle** - In learning, a small number of topics will build most of the subject. 
 
>Combination: 
>**First principles builds the pillars.** **Pareto filters each pillar down to its high-leverage 20%—multiple topics, not just one.** **Cognitive science encodes that selected 20% for durable, flexible mastery.**
 
# Usage
---
1. **First principles** → Identify the irreducible **pillars** of the subject.  
   *Example: recon, construction, manipulation, protection.*
 
2. **Pareto within each pillar** → For every pillar, ask:  
   > What is the ~20% of topics in this pillar that produces ~80% of the functional capability or understanding for that pillar?  
   This yields **multiple high-yield topics per pillar**, not just one.  
   These topics are the ones that other topics build upon, or that recur most often in real-world application.
 
3. **Combine across pillars** → The total selected material is the union of each pillar’s 20%.  
   That total may be more than one topic per pillar, but it’s still dramatically smaller than learning everything.
 
4. **Apply cognitive techniques** → Active recall, spaced repetition, interleaving, and elaboration to encode that selected material deeply.
 
---
 
### Example:
 
| Pillar            | High-yield topics (the ~20% of that pillar)                         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Info recon        | Intel cycle(10%), OSINT(2%), source evaluation(8%)                  |
| Info construction | Forgery(10%), cover identity(4%), document fabrication(6%)          |
| Info manipulation | Elicitation(6%), pretexting(4%), social engineering frameworks(10%) |
| Info protection   | Opsec cycle(10%), counter-surveillance(6%), digital hygiene(4%)     |
 
That’s still a small fraction of the total tradecraft field, but it covers the load-bearing core of each pillar.
 
**Summary**:
> **First principles → pillars → Pareto-filtered topics per pillar → active recall/spacing/interleaving**
 
**Important caveat**:
This strategy is an excellent **selection and structure method**, while you learn the topics that unlock or explain the most other topics, so later learning becomes easier and faster, you have to actually test if the 20% is outputting the 80% in practice this is covered via mixed interleaved practices + compressed feedback loop or customized feedback loop depending on your learning architecture/study system.
 
**To be truly efficient**, follow this guideline:
- **Feedback loops**  
    Testing whether your selected 20% is actually producing the 80% in practice.
- **Iteration**  
    If a selected topic isn’t unlocking other topics as expected, you revise the pillar or the filter.
- **Encoding work**  
    The selected topics still have to be actively recalled, spaced, applied, and elaborated—otherwise they decay.
- **Real-world calibration**  
    Especially in fields like tradecraft, the real 20% may only reveal itself through operational experience, not just theory.
