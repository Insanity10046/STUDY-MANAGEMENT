## The Feedback Loop
> A feedback loop is the cycle that turns practice into learning. It is the mechanism by which an error corrects your mental model.
### Core definition
> **Attempt → observe outcome → compare with expected/correct → detect mismatch → correct mental model → repeat**
In learning science, this is called **error-based learning** or **predictive error correction**.

Your brain makes a prediction. The world gives a result. The gap between prediction and result is the **error signal**. That signal updates your internal model.
 
---
 
## The loop in detail
 
| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Attempt | You solve a problem, make a judgment, or perform a task. |
| 2. Outcome | You receive a result — correct answer, model answer, expert critique, real-world consequence. |
| 3. Compare | You compare what you did with what should have been done. |
| 4. Detect mismatch | You identify the specific gap or error. |
| 5. Correct model | You refine your summary, rule, or schema at the point of failure. |
| 6. Repeat | You attempt again with the updated model. |
 
This is the engine of the learning process.

---
 
## Error signal
 
The error signal is the information that tells you **where** and **how** you were wrong.
 
- In math: “The proof broke at this step.”
- In law: “The model answer says no duty existed here.”
- In forensics: “The chain of custody was broken before the sample was tested.”
 
A strong, specific error signal makes learning fast.  
A weak or vague error signal makes learning slow.
 
---
 
## Feedback delay
 
Feedback delay is the time between your action and the error signal.
 
- **Short delay:** You solve an algebra problem and immediately check the answer.
- **Long delay:** You submit a legal analysis and get feedback a week later.
- **Very long delay:** You make a tradecraft decision and only discover the mistake months later.
 
Why delay matters:
 
- Long delay weakens the connection between action and correction.
- You may repeat the same error many times.
- It becomes harder to know which specific decision caused the failure.
 
So the goal is to **shorten the delay** as much as possible.
 
---
 
## Compressed feedback loop
 
A compressed feedback loop is when you artificially shorten the delay and increase the clarity of the error signal.
 
Instead of waiting for real-world outcomes, you use:
 
- model answers
- past exam solutions
- expert critique
- simulations
- mock trials
- after-action reviews
- proficiency tests
- red-team exercises
- checklists
 
Example:
 
> Real legal feedback may take months.  
> A compressed loop uses a past exam question and a model answer, compared immediately after your attempt.
 
That creates a strong error signal now, not months later.

---
**IMPORTANT CAVEAT**:
- If that loop is weak, the whole study system slows down.  
- If that loop is tight and honest, the study system works very fast — even in messy domains.
---
 
## One-sentence version
 
> A feedback loop is the cycle of acting, detecting error, and correcting your mental model, and the speed and clarity of that cycle determine how fast you learn.
