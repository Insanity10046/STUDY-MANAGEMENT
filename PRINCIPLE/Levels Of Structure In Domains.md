# Levels Of Structures (In Domains)
 
> **The learning method must mirror the epistemic structure of the domain.**  
> Well-structured domains reward compressed generative rules and high-volume problem solving.  
> Ill-structured domains reward case-based reasoning, conditional knowledge, and simulated judgment.  
> The less structured the domain, the less a rule summary can substitute for pattern recognition and contextual decision-making.
 
---
 
### Explanation
 
Domains fall on a continuum.
 
| Domain type | Features | Example | Primary learning object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Well-structured** | Clear rules, objective right/wrong, strong feedback | Algebra, formal syntax, logic | Generative rule schema + problems |
| **Semi-structured** | Rules exist but exceptions and context matter | Statutory law, forensic protocols | Rules + conditional cases |
| **Ill-structured** | Ambiguous facts, multiple valid answers, delayed/noisy feedback | Legal argument, tradecraft, clinical judgment | Cases, scenarios, decision-making under uncertainty |
 
The more ill-structured the domain, the more knowledge becomes **conditional and case-based**, not rule-based.
 
---
 
### Cognitive science basis
 
- **Transfer-appropriate processing**  
  Practice must resemble the final task. If the final task is arguing a case or making a field judgment, then solving only rule-based problems is insufficient.
 
- **Cognitive flexibility theory**  
  Ill-structured domains require multiple cases and perspectives to prevent reductive bias — the false belief that a simple rule captures the whole domain.
 
- **Schema theory**  
  In well-structured domains, a small schema can generate many correct solutions. In ill-structured domains, the schema is just a scaffold; the real expertise lives in case libraries and decision procedures.
 
---
 
### Consequences for method design
 
1. **Well-structured domain**
   - Broad rule-based schema
   - Mixed problem sets
   - Fast closed-form feedback
   - Fading cheat sheet works very well
 
2. **Ill-structured domain**
   - Schema is expert-sourced
   - Add case bank / scenario bank
   - Use model answers, expert critique, simulations
   - Cheat sheet must include conditional decision rules, not just facts
   - Mindmap should map case discriminators and decision trees
 
---
 
### One-line version
 
> **In structured domains, learn the rule and drill it. In unstructured domains, learn the case and judge it. Match the method to the domain’s level of structure.**
 
That is the principle behind adapting study systems to different domains.
