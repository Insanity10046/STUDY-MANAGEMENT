## Knowledge Type Principle
 
> **Different knowledge types require different practice architectures.**  
> Declarative knowledge is built by organizing and retrieving.  
> Procedural knowledge is built by doing with feedback.  
> Conditional knowledge is built by comparing and discriminating.  
> Tacit knowledge is built by situated experience.  
> **Match the learning activity to the knowledge type you are trying to build.**
 
---
 
### The four knowledge types
 
| Knowledge type | Definition | Example | Primary learning method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Declarative** | Knowing *that* — facts, rules, schemas | Algebra: inverse operations; Law: negligence elements | Schema construction, retrieval practice, elaboration |
| **Procedural** | Knowing *how* — fluent action | Solving systems; cross-examining a witness | Deliberate practice, drills, simulations, fading |
| **Conditional** | Knowing *when and why* | When to use substitution vs elimination; when duty is weak in pure economic loss | Interleaved cases, analogical comparison, discrimination |
| **Tacit** | Knowing *in context* — hard to articulate | Reading a room in tradecraft; sensing contamination risk in a lab | Cognitive apprenticeship, role-play, situated feedback |
 
---
 
### How they relate
 
Declarative knowledge is the **scaffold**.  
Procedural knowledge is the **skill**.  
Conditional knowledge is the **selector** — it tells you which skill to use when.  
Tacit knowledge is the **feel** — it operates below full articulation.
 
Cognitive science says most real competence requires all four, but they develop through different mechanisms.
 
---
 
### Cognitive basis
 
- **ACT-R** — Anderson distinguishes declarative and procedural knowledge. Procedural knowledge emerges through repeated use of declarative knowledge, compiled into production rules.
 
- **Transfer-appropriate processing** — practice must resemble the final task. If the final task is doing, not just knowing, then practice must involve doing.
 
- **Cognitive flexibility theory** — conditional knowledge requires seeing the same concept across multiple cases; otherwise you develop a reductive, brittle understanding.
 
- **Cognitive apprenticeship** — tacit knowledge is learned by modeling, coaching, and reflection, not by reading summaries.
 
---
 
### Consequences for most study systems
 
1. **Declarative knowledge**
   - Build expert-sourced schemas.
   - Use retrieval practice and concept maps.
   - Write final summary only after consolidation.
 
2. **Procedural knowledge**
   - Add timed drills and simulations.
   - Fade cheat sheets to force automaticity.
   - Use after-action reviews.
 
3. **Conditional knowledge**
   - Interleave cases from the start.
   - Compare analogous cases/scenarios.
   - Refine condition–action rules after errors.
 
4. **Tacit knowledge**
   - Use role-play, moot court, lab practice, field exercises.
   - Reflect after performance: what cue did I miss? what would I do differently?
   - Seek expert feedback in context.
 
---
 
### Common failure modes
 
| If you only… | You develop… | But miss… |
|---|---|---|
| Read and summarize | Declarative knowledge | Skill, timing, judgment |
| Drill without understanding | Brittle procedural knowledge | Transfer, adaptation |
| Memorize rules without cases | Rigid declarative knowledge | Conditional wisdom |
| Study only in classroom | Declarative/procedural | Tacit contextual feel |
 
---
 
### One-line version
 
> **Learn *that* before *how*, but don’t stop at *that*. Build *how* by doing, build *when* by comparing, and build *feel* by being there.**
 
That is the principle behind choosing different practice types for different knowledge types.
